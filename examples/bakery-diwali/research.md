## Audience

Working parents in the city, 28-45, who swap sweet boxes with neighbours, relatives and colleagues in the Diwali
week. They shop on their phones in the evening, after work, and decide fast [S1]. They care that a gift feels
personal and home-made, not like a factory box from a supermarket shelf [S2].

## Problem in their words

- "Diwali se pehle time hi nahi milta, gift ka socha bhi nahi" (no time before Diwali to even think about gifts) [S2]
- "Har saal wahi dabba, wahi soan papdi" (every year the same box, the same sweets) [S3]
- "Ghar ka bana kuch dena hai, par banane ka time kahan" (want to give something home-made, no time to make it) [S2]

## Competitors' angles

- Supermarket gift hampers: big boxes, discounts, corporate look; rarely say who made them [S4]
- Large sweet-shop chains: tradition and variety; long queues in the Diwali week [S3]
- Home bakers on social media: personal and home-made, but small batches that sell out early [S5]

## Claims allowed

- C1: The Diwali box holds 12 hand-made kaju-pista cookies. [brief]
- C2: The box costs Rs 499. [brief]
- C3: The cookies are made by hand. [brief]
- C4: Orders are delivered in the city within 2 days. [brief]
- C5: Many buyers want gifts that feel home-made rather than factory-packed. [S2]

## Angle

Stand on "ghar jaisa" (like home-made) without the effort: a busy parent finds a hand-made box in time, and a
neighbour's first bite says the rest. Show the hands that make it; say the three brief facts plainly.
